# Problem statement

Authentication is a sequence of obligations, not a single successful credential comparison. An application may incorrectly allow access before consent, after logout, after token expiry, or before identity verification. A service may accept a duplicate application even when authentication is correct.

IDShield makes these mistakes observable in an independent fictional scholarship service. Finite automata enforce modeled event ordering; conventional guards handle clocks and credentials; a claim store handles record uniqueness. The student can inspect the same transition relation that determines runtime decisions and introduce a faulty model to obtain a shortest counterexample.

Success is defined as correct decisions under the explicitly modeled rules and bounded tests, not prevention of all identity fraud. No real citizen data is processed. Scholarship criteria are invented teaching rules, not actual government eligibility requirements.
